# Streaming Media: Cross-Company Operating and Valuation Comparison

**Analyst: Jiarong Xu**  
**Retrospective baseline: 31 December 2025**  
**Explicit forecasts: 2026–2030 | Stable period: 2031 onward**  
**Units: USD millions, except shares, per-share amounts and percentages**

## Purpose and principal findings

This report compares how Netflix, Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) convert their business assumptions into operating profit, investment, free cash flow and shareholder value. It synthesizes the three accompanying models without replacing their company-specific assumptions.

The comparison supports three distinct analytical conclusions:

- **Netflix is the streaming-focused operating benchmark.** Its forecast links regional membership and monetization to profit growth. Content investment and estimated membership data remain important limits on the cash-flow interpretation.
- **Paramount is particularly sensitive to investment calibration.** DTC growth does not fully offset legacy TV contraction in the Base revenue forecast. An immediate return to historical asset-intensity ratios creates a large first-year investment requirement.
- **WBD illustrates the interaction of leverage and accounting-based reinvestment.** Included financial claims absorb much of operating value. An EBITDA-linked asset schedule can turn weaker operations into apparent investment releases, explaining why Bear can exceed Base.

These are conclusions about the businesses and the delivered models, not a ranking of expected stock returns. Per-share values cannot establish which company is cheaper without consistent market prices, claim definitions and accounting adjustments.

## 1. What is comparable?

The models share an end-2025 baseline, five explicit forecast years, year-end discounting and a stable year beginning in 2031. All use subsequently published FY2025 disclosures and archived March 2026 Workspace inputs. They are retrospective analyses, rather than investment decisions reconstructed from information available at year-end 2025. Subsequent deal consideration, financing and unmodelled synergies are excluded.

| Dimension | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue forecast | DTC, TV Media and Film growth | Regional average memberships × monthly ARM × 12 | Streaming subscribers × annual ARPU, Studios growth and Networks revenue lines |
| Operating-profit build | Revenue-based COGS and SG&A estimates | Consolidated expense assumptions applied to regional revenue | Adjusted EBITDA less D&A and estimated recurring SBC |
| Base forecast operating tax | 16.2176% historical ETR proxy | 14%, 14%, 14.5%, 15%, 15% | 20%, 20%, 20.5%, 21%, 21% |
| Operating investment | Historical-average OA/revenue and OL/revenue ratios | Forecast OA/revenue and OL/revenue paths | OA/adjusted EBITDA and OL/adjusted EBITDA paths, recalibrated to 2025 |
| Material reporting issue | 2025 Predecessor/Successor accounting break | Standardized historical series; estimated memberships/ARM from 2025 | 2022 pro forma operating comparison; normalized profit differs from GAAP |

OA means selected operating assets; OL means selected operating liabilities. These classifications are not fully standardized across companies. Neither a common statutory tax rate nor a common investment method is imposed. The archived WACCs also differ in date and company basis; they are not a controlled same-day risk comparison.

Historical cash-flow references can be compared for context, with reporting-scope qualifications. Forecast margins, cash-flow conversion and valuation differences reflect both business assumptions and modelling choices. Adjusted OIBDA, adjusted EBITDA, GAAP operating income and model operating-profit proxies are not interchangeable measures.

## 2. Business transition and operating evidence

### Netflix: scale, monetization and operating leverage

Reported revenue increased from 39,000.97 in 2024 to 45,183.04 in 2025, while operating income increased from 10,417.61 to 13,326.60. The Base forecast assumes further membership and monetization growth, with expense leverage through 2029 and expense ratios held constant in 2030. International markets contribute growth alongside the mature UCAN business.

This creates a relatively direct operating narrative, but the membership × ARM decomposition is estimated from 2025 onward. Actual disclosed regional revenue remains separate from those estimates. ARM captures pricing, plan mix, advertising and other monetization effects; it is not simply a subscription sticker price. Netflix's content assets and liabilities also mean that strong accounting profit is not sufficient evidence of equally strong cash conversion.

### Paramount: streaming improvement against television contraction

The FY2025 reported-period sums show DTC revenue of 8,584 and adjusted OIBDA of 230, compared with 2024 DTC revenue of 7,632 and OIBDA of (497). TV Media remains much larger, with 2025 segment revenue of 17,059 and OIBDA of 3,695. The streaming improvement therefore needs to be assessed alongside the scale and decline of television.

The August 2025 transaction limits comparability: the period sums join different accounting bases. The model retains consolidated revenue of 28,891, distinct from supplemental pro forma revenue of 29,394. Its external segment bases are DTC 8,584, TV 17,009 and Film 3,298, summing to 28,891 after removing internal sales. Forecast cost allocations do not establish independently observed segment margins.

### WBD: improving Streaming within a contracting group

Streaming revenue increased from 10,313 in 2024 to 10,876 in 2025, while Studios revenue increased from 11,607 to 12,619. Networks revenue fell from 20,175 to 17,656. After Corporate and inter-segment eliminations, consolidated revenue fell from 39,321 to 37,296 and group adjusted EBITDA fell from 9,032 to 8,744.

Streaming growth alone therefore does not resolve the group cash-flow question. The forecast includes Corporate costs, eliminations, D&A and an estimated recurring SBC cost of 2.0136% of revenue. The resulting normalized operating-profit proxy is not GAAP EBIT, because other adjusted-EBITDA exclusions are not separately forecast. The preserved D&A path also assumes substantial normalization from the actual 2025 ratio.

## 3. Cash generation: observed amounts and model estimates

### Historical cash-flow context

| FY2025 measure | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue base used in the analysis | 28,891.00 | 45,183.04 | 37,296.00 |
| Reported operating cash flow | 649.00 | 10,149.27 | 4,319.00 |
| PP&E purchases / cash capex, spending magnitude | 296.00 | 688.22 | 1,231.00 |
| Operating cash flow less those purchases | 353.00 | 9,461.05 | 3,088.00 |

Paramount's cash flows cover continuing operations and sum its Predecessor and Successor periods. The Netflix and WBD figures follow their reported consolidated cash-flow presentations. These amounts provide context; they are not tax-, financing- and scope-harmonized unlevered FCFF. Content payments can already be included in operating cash flow, so small PP&E spending does not imply little content investment.

The delivered forecasts instead estimate net investment through operating assets:

$$
NOA_t=OA_t-OL_t,\qquad FCFF_t=NOPAT_t-(NOA_t-NOA_{t-1})
$$

NOA is net operating assets; NOPAT is the model's after-tax operating-profit measure. Higher NOA represents estimated net investment and reduces FCFF. Lower NOA creates a modelled investment release. No separate depreciation add-back and capex deduction are appended to the same net-investment calculation. Changes caused by goodwill, impairments, purchase accounting, FX or other noncash items can weaken its interpretation as actual cash spending.

### Base forecast comparison

| Model metric | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2025 revenue base | 28,891.00 | 45,183.04 | 37,296.00 |
| 2030 forecast revenue | 27,619.75 | 62,955.29 | 41,132.50 |
| 2025–2030 revenue CAGR | -0.9% | 6.9% | 2.0% |
| 2030 model NOPAT / revenue | 8.4% | 28.3% | 8.6% |
| 2030 model FCFF / revenue | 8.5% | 24.6% | 6.3% |
| 2026 change in NOA | 7,297.70 | 2,515.18 | -1,688.77 |
| 2026 model FCFF | -4,848.67 | 10,508.98 | 4,661.26 |
| 2030 model FCFF | 2,336.15 | 15,517.58 | 2,596.37 |

CAGR is compound annual growth over the five years from 2025 to 2030. Margins use consolidated model revenue. These are forecast diagnostics under different expense, tax and investment definitions, not audited comparisons of underlying profitability.

**Netflix's Base forecast combines revenue growth with higher after-tax operating margins.** The 2026 NOA increase of about 2,515 is smaller than NOPAT of about 13,024, leaving positive FCFF. Its operating narrative is strongest when membership, monetization, content costs and the assumed asset-intensity path are considered together.

**Paramount's first-year FCFF is dominated by investment calibration.** NOA rises from 19,436 to 26,733.70 as the model immediately applies historical-average ratios. Subtracting that increase from NOPAT of 2,449.03 produces FCFF of (4,848.67). This is an assumption about the asset base required to operate, not a disclosed cash-spending commitment. Under the same Base operating case, holding 2025 ratios gives $23.58 per share, versus $16.94 under the default and $17.68 under gradual transition. No alternative is selected because it gives a preferred price.

**WBD's 2026 FCFF benefits from a modelled asset release.** NOPAT of 2,972.49 exceeds neither Netflix's absolute profit nor its margin, yet FCFF reaches 4,661.26 because NOA falls by 1,688.77. Goodwill and acquisition-related assets make it particularly important not to interpret every accounting asset reduction as realizable cash.

## 4. From operating value to shareholder value

| Base model component | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WACC | 6.5636% | 9.8412% | 8.1842% |
| Perpetual growth | 0.5% | 2.5% | 2.0% |
| PV of explicit FCFF | 3,714.81 | 50,239.98 | 12,591.86 |
| PV of terminal value | 26,545.66 | 146,488.07 | 24,904.65 |
| Operating value | 30,260.47 | 196,728.05 | 37,496.51 |
| Included financial assets | 3,366.00 | 9,062.36 | 5,263.00 |
| Included debt | 13,658.00 | 14,462.84 | 33,250.00 |
| Included NCI, book-value proxies | 1,194.00 | 0.00 | 1,247.00 |
| Equity reference | 18,774.47 | 191,327.57 | 8,262.51 |
| Period-end shares, millions | 1,108.00 | 4,222.16 | 2,480.00 |
| Base value per share | $16.94 | $45.32 | $3.33 |
| Terminal share of operating value | 87.7% | 74.5% | 66.4% |

The equity bridge is:

$$
E=V_{operations}+IncludedFinancialAssets-IncludedDebt-IncludedNCI
$$

Here $E$ is the model equity reference. Paramount financial assets total 3,366; Netflix cash and short-term investments total 9,062.36; WBD cash and included investments total 5,263. WBD NCI includes both equity and redeemable components, and its debt includes finance leases. Netflix's provider NCI difference is zero. Other claim classifications, leases and dilution assumptions remain as documented in each report. The bridge is not a complete market-value appraisal of every obligation.

### A mechanical illustration of equity sensitivity

Define **included net claims** as included debt plus NCI, less included financial assets. This is an analytical bridge measure, not reported net debt or a debt/EBITDA ratio. If those claims and share counts are held fixed, a dollar change in operating value passes directly to equity:

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{E}=\frac{V_{operations}}{E}\times\frac{\Delta V_{operations}}{V_{operations}}
$$

| Mechanical Base-case illustration | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Included net claims | 11,486.00 | 5,400.48 | 29,234.00 |
| Included net claims / operating value | 38.0% | 2.7% | 78.0% |
| Equity decline if operating value falls 10% | 16.1% | 10.3% | 45.4% |
| Per-share reference after that mechanical shock | $14.21 | $40.66 | $1.82 |

The same 10% reduction in operating value therefore has a much larger percentage effect on WBD equity. This is arithmetic under fixed claims, not a stress forecast: an actual deterioration could also change cash balances, financing terms, claim values and dilution. It does not establish default probabilities or comparable borrowing costs.

## 5. Scenarios and terminal assumptions

| Operating scenario, USD per share | Paramount | Netflix | WBD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Base | 16.94 | 45.32 | 3.33 |
| Bull | 23.53 | 52.84 | 5.07 |
| Bear | 9.76 | 32.46 | 4.03 |

Each company uses its own scenario paths while holding its own WACC and perpetual growth fixed. The scenarios are neither aligned percentage shocks nor assigned probabilities. Their ranges cannot be used to rank risk or expected returns directly.

**WBD's scenario inversion is informative.** Its Bear case closes 2030 with NOA of about 39,818, versus 65,883 in Base. Lower EBITDA drives a smaller operating asset base and large forecast investment releases. This can offset lower profits. The higher Bear equity estimate therefore does not mean weaker operations are economically preferable; it shows how heavily value depends on whether those inferred releases are cash-realizable. Bull also requires more investment, so stronger near-term profit need not produce higher near-term FCFF.

**Terminal value requires growth and investment to agree.** At fixed final-year margins and operating asset intensity, the models use:

$$
FCFF_{2031}=NOPAT_{2030}(1+g)-gNOA_{2030}
$$

$$
TV_{2030}=\frac{FCFF_{2031}}{WACC-g},\qquad WACC>g
$$

The first term grows operating profit; the second funds the corresponding growth in operating assets. Terminal value is measured at end-2030 and discounted five years. The numerator begins in 2031, after the five explicit forecast cash flows.

For WBD, Base 2030 NOPAT divided by closing NOA is about 5.36%, below WACC of 8.1842%. Holding those ratios fixed, faster stable growth requires investment at a low implied return and reduces value. This accounting-based ratio is not independently measured incremental cash ROIC. Its level can be distorted by goodwill and purchase-accounting balances. Netflix and Paramount's delivered Base sensitivity tables instead show higher values as perpetual growth rises within their tested ranges.

Paramount has the largest terminal contribution at 87.7%, compared with Netflix's 74.5% and WBD's 66.4%. These shares measure the timing of model value, not the probability of forecast success. A low perpetual growth assumption does not by itself make a valuation conservative. In Paramount and WBD, the group-wide stable-growth closure also assumes that legacy network contraction eventually stabilizes; it is not evidence that the transition is complete in 2031.

## 6. Comparative conclusion and useful extensions

The central distinction is how each business converts its revenue path into cash after maintaining and expanding its operating base. Netflix's delivered Base case has the clearest growth-and-margin story. Paramount's result places substantial weight on the operating asset intensity assumed after its accounting reset. WBD combines business-transition risk with a thin model equity residual and an investment schedule that can generate counterintuitive cash releases.

Those findings do not justify changing assumptions merely to obtain a desired ordering. The most useful next development would be a separately documented cash-investment bridge: content cash spending and amortization, content liabilities, organic capex, working capital, acquisition-related assets and other noncash changes. It would also help to develop cash-tax schedules and more complete claim/dilution valuations. Any such extension should be reconciled to the existing model rather than silently substituted for it.

For a future investment comparison, an additional step would be to align dated market prices, split-adjusted shares, financial-claim definitions and the information set used for discount rates. This report makes no current buy/sell recommendation and does not incorporate subsequent transaction economics.
